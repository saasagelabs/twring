How to call your AI agent from your phone

Install Twring on your iPhone or Android phone, connect the agent you already run on your computer or server, and press Call. You talk, it answers out loud, and you can cut in at any time. No phone number or phone plan is needed, and your agent keeps its own model, memory and tools.

It works with Claude Code, Codex, Hermes, and agents you can reach at their own web address, such as OpenClaw. Claude, ChatGPT and other AI apps can't take a live call, but they can ring your phone and message you. When a long job is done or needs your OK, your agent can ring you too, full screen even when your phone is locked.

How do I set it up?

  1. Get an invite and install the app. Leave your email and we send the steps for your phone. On iPhone, Twring comes through TestFlight, Apple's app for early releases. On Android, it's an APK from twring.com, not Google Play; if Android or Play Protect asks, allow the install.
  2. Sign in with the email your invite went to. Use Google, or Apple on iPhone.
  3. Connect your agent. In the app, tap Connect Agent, then On my computer or server.
    • Claude Code or Codex: paste the short message the app gives you into your agent and approve the command it runs, or tap Scan a code on my computer, run the command it shows in your project folder, and scan the code it prints. It needs Node.js 20 or newer. Step by step: Claude Code, Codex.
    • Hermes: on the machine where it runs, run hermes plugins install twring and hermes twring link, scan the code in the app, then start hermes gateway. Step by step: Hermes.
  4. Press Call. Your agent appears in the app. Press Call and talk normally. Talk over it at any time and it stops speaking.
  5. Make sure rings can reach you. In Twring, open Settings: Calls should say Ready. Set quiet hours if you don't want rings at night.

What happens on a call?

Twring turns your speech into text and hands it to your agent, the same as if you had typed. Your agent works in its own folder or server, with its own model, memory, tools and permissions. Twring never runs its tools. The reply is spoken while it's still being written, so you hear the start of the answer before the end exists. The full walkthrough.

Things said on a call are easy to lose once you hang up, so Twring gives your agent a short guide: confirm each request in one short line, write it down with the time where the agent runs (never passwords or keys), work through the open items oldest first after the call, and report each result in your Twring chat.

  • No recordings. Twring listens only while a call is on, and never records or keeps your audio.
  • Keys stay encrypted. An agent's key is stored encrypted and is never shown again.
  • No phone number. Calls and rings come over the internet through the Twring app, so there's no phone provider account and no per-minute bill.
  • Open source. The app and the computer tool are Apache-2.0; the server, relay and self-host kit are AGPL-3.0. You can run Twring on your own server with a Deepgram or xAI speech account.

What are the limits?

  • Invite only, outside the app stores. We let people in in waves so every call works well. TestFlight or an APK is one step more than an app store.
  • Free while we launch, with a daily allowance. Each person has a daily amount of calling time, which starts again at midnight UTC, and each call has a maximum length. Each agent can ring you at most 6 times an hour. See pricing.
  • Your computer has to be on. An agent on your laptop can take a call only while the laptop is on and awake, with Twring running on it. If it sleeps, the agent shows as offline in the app. An agent on a server doesn't have this problem.
  • Voice is not a screen. A call is good for talking through a plan, starting a job or answering a question while you walk. Reading a long diff or an exact error message is still a job for a screen.

Is it for me?

Skip it if all you want is a quiet alert when a job ends; a simple notification app does that. Set it up if you run Claude Code, Codex, Hermes or OpenClaw on a computer or server, want to talk to it from your phone without buying a number, and want it to ring you when it needs your OK.

Questions

Do I need a phone number or a Twilio account to call my AI agent?

No. Calls and rings go over the internet to the Twring app. You'd only need a phone number and a phone provider if you wanted your agent to dial ordinary phone numbers, which Twring doesn't do.

Is Twring free?

It's free while we launch, and nothing is ever charged without your OK. While it's free, each person has a daily amount of calling time, which starts again at midnight UTC, and each agent can ring you at most 6 times an hour. You still pay your AI provider for your agent's usage, as you do today.

Can I call Claude or ChatGPT itself?

Not live. AI apps like Claude and ChatGPT can't take an incoming call, but once you add Twring to them they can ring your phone and send you messages. For a live call, connect Claude Code, Codex or Hermes on your computer.

How is Twring different from agentring?

As of 26 November 2026, agentring is an open source app (Apache-2.0) for Android and iPhone in which a realtime speech model does the talking. With Twring you talk to your agent itself: Twring only handles the voice, and the agent you connected does all the thinking and acting. Twring vs agentring goes through the differences.

Why didn't my phone ring?

In Twring, open Settings and check that Calls says Ready. Then check Do Not Disturb and quiet hours, that your phone is signed in to the same account as the agent, the limit of 6 rings an hour, and that the computer running your agent is awake. Every check, in order.
