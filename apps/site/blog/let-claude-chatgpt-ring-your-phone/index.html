Let Claude or ChatGPT ring your phone when it needs you

Add https://api.twring.com/mcp as a custom connector in Claude, ChatGPT or another AI app that allows one, sign in with the same account as your Twring app, and allow it. From then on that AI can ring your phone with a short reason, send messages and files to its Twring chat, and read the replies you send it there.

What this does

Twring is a phone app for calling and texting your AI agents. AI chat apps like Claude and ChatGPT can't take incoming calls, but they can reach out to you. Once you connect Twring to one of them, it gets a small set of tools:

  • Ring your phone with a short reason (at most 140 characters) when it needs you. Twring reads the reason aloud when you answer, then opens that AI's chat so you can reply by voice or text.
  • Send you a message, with up to five files by link, without ringing. Good for results and anything that can wait.
  • Read your replies in that chat the next time it checks.
  • Check whether a ring can get through right now, for example if you have Do Not Disturb or quiet hours on.

The ring is full screen, even on a locked phone. It goes over the internet to the Twring app, so no phone number or telephony account is involved.

What's a connector?

A connector is how you add tools to an AI app. Under the hood most use MCP, the standard way AI apps add tools. Twring's connector is an MCP server at one address: https://api.twring.com/mcp. Twring is also listed in the official MCP registry.

Add it to your AI app

First, get an invite and install Twring on your iPhone or Android phone, and sign in. Then:

  1. Claude: Settings, Connectors, Add custom connector. Paste https://api.twring.com/mcp, then sign in and allow.
  2. ChatGPT: Settings, Apps and Connectors (or Developer mode, Create). Paste the same address, then sign in and allow.
  3. Grok, Cursor and other apps: look for Connectors in the app's settings, add a custom connector with the same address, then sign in and allow.

Sign in the same way you sign in to the Twring app (Google or Apple), with the same account. Rings go to the account, not to whichever phone is in your hand, so if you sign in to the connector with a different account than your phone uses, the ring has nowhere to go. Full steps are on the connector page.

Asking it to ring you

Once connected, just say what you want in plain words. For example:

  • "Research these three suppliers, and ring me when you have a recommendation."
  • "Draft the reply, send it to my Twring chat, and only ring me if you need a decision."
  • "If anything in this report looks wrong, ring me."

When it rings, you hear its reason, then land in its Twring chat. Reply there by voice or text. The AI reads your answer the next time it checks for replies, so it may not react the very second you answer.

A good habit for the AI is to message by default and ring only when you must know or decide now. If it rings too often, tell it so in the chat.

What it can and can't see

A connected AI app gets only what you write or say in its own Twring chat. It can't read your other Twring chats, your other agents or your calls. Twring never records or keeps call audio; the privacy notice has the details.

To remove it, open Twring and go to Settings, Connected AI apps, Remove. It can't reach you again unless you allow it.

What it doesn't do

You can't place a live voice call to Claude or ChatGPT from Twring: chat apps don't take incoming calls. The connector is for the AI reaching you, and for you replying in its chat.

If you want live two-way calls, connect an agent you run yourself, such as Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI or Hermes. Those can ring you too, and you can call them back.

Other ways to get a ring or a ping

There are other routes, and some may suit you better:

  • A real phone call over the phone network. Telephony companies offer their own MCP servers. As of November 2026, Plivo's MCP docs describe one for its voice and messaging services. That route uses a telephony account and phone numbers, which makes sense if you need to call ordinary phone numbers.
  • A push notification. If a buzz is enough and you never need to talk back, a simple notification service may be all you need.

Twring fits when you want a ring you can't miss, a reply channel in the same place, and no phone number or carrier setup.

When it doesn't ring

  • Check the account. The connector and the phone app must be signed in to the same Twring account.
  • Check Twring's settings. Do Not Disturb and quiet hours in Twring stop rings. Notifications must be on for Twring.
  • Check it's still connected. Look in Settings, Connected AI apps. If it isn't listed, add the connector again.

Questions

Does my phone need a phone number for this?

No. The ring reaches the Twring app over the internet, so there is no phone number or telephony account involved.

Which AI apps can use the Twring connector?

Claude, ChatGPT, and other apps that let you add a custom connector in their settings, such as Grok and Cursor. They all use the same address, https://api.twring.com/mcp.

Why didn't my phone ring?

Most often the connector was signed in with a different account than your phone, or Do Not Disturb or quiet hours are on in Twring. Rings go to the account, not the device.
