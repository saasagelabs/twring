Approving your agent's risky actions by voice, away from your desk

It depends on the agent: with Hermes, a risky command shows up on your phone as an approval card, you say yes or no or tap, and silence means no. Claude Code and Codex don't ask mid-call through Twring; instead they run with safe limits you choose when you connect them (read-only by default), and they can ring you when they need your OK.

Why approvals matter more away from your desk

Agents that can run commands, edit files or change systems usually stop and ask before anything risky. That is fine at your desk. On a walk, it means work sits waiting until you get back.

Twring is a phone app for calling the agents you already run. It doesn't add its own rules about what your agent may do. Your agent keeps its own permissions, and Twring never runs its tools. What Twring changes is how the question reaches you, and that is different for each agent. So here they are one at a time.

Hermes: ask and answer on the call

Hermes has its own approval system, and the Twring plugin carries it to your phone:

  1. Hermes wants to run a command that needs your OK.
  2. An approval card appears on your phone, with the command and Hermes's reason.
  3. On a call, Twring reads a short fixed line: Hermes wants to run a command; say yes or no, or tap on your phone. The card's details are shown, not read aloud.
  4. Say "yes" or "no", or tap. Your answer goes back through Hermes's own approval system.

Three rules make this safe:

  • Only you can approve. The plugin never approves anything for you.
  • Silence means no. If you don't answer, the request is denied.
  • Long commands are deny-only from the phone. If a command is too long to show on the card, you can decline it from your phone but approve it only at the computer.

Because the card shows the full detail and the spoken line doesn't, glance at the screen before you say yes to anything you can't undo.

Claude Code: safe by default, edits only if you allow them

On a Twring call, Claude Code doesn't stop to ask you questions about permissions. Instead, the Twring connector starts it in a mode where anything it isn't already allowed to do is simply not done. By default it can read, search and look things up on the web, but not change your files.

If you want it to edit files on calls, you decide that up front, when you connect it, by adding --claude-allow-edits to the command. That lets it edit and write files in that folder.

Claude Code can also ring your phone when it needs your OK or a job is done, so you hear about it even when you aren't on a call. See getting a call when Claude Code finishes or needs your OK.

Codex: a read-only sandbox unless you allow writing

On a Twring call, Codex works in a read-only sandbox (a fenced-off space that limits what it can touch): it can read your files but not change them. To let it write in that folder, add --codex-sandbox workspace-write when you connect it.

Like Claude Code, Codex can ring your phone when a job is done or it needs your OK.

Good habits for approving by voice

  • Ask the agent to say what it wants first. One plain sentence, such as "I need to run the database migration; it adds one column and is hard to undo", makes yes or no easy.
  • Check names and numbers. Speech recognition can mishear. For anything costly or hard to undo, have the agent read back the exact target, and check the card on your screen.
  • Grant less, not more. Start with the read-only defaults, and widen them only for the folders and agents where you need it.
  • "No" is a fine answer. A denied request is not a failure. A good agent offers another way.

Other ways to approve from your phone

If you only want to tap through permission prompts in text, an official remote-control app for your agent may suit you better than a voice call. As of November 2026, Anthropic's Claude Code docs describe Remote Control: you drive a Claude Code session running on your computer from the Claude app on your phone, and it can send a push notification when it needs a decision from you. If typing is what you want, that is the better pick. Twring is for when you'd rather talk, or want your agent to ring you instead of waiting in a chat.

Twring never records or keeps call audio. See the privacy notice, and support if something doesn't behave as described.

Questions

Can Twring approve an action for me?

No. Twring never approves anything on its own. With Hermes, only you approve, by voice or tap, and silence means no.

Can Claude Code ask me for permission in the middle of a call?

Not through Twring. On a call it runs with the limits you set when you connected it: reading and searching by default, and editing only if you added --claude-allow-edits.

What happens if I miss a Hermes approval request?

It counts as no, and the command does not run. Silence means no.
